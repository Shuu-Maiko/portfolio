## What is Cron

Cron is a **daemon** (background service) that **executes scheduled tasks** at specified times or intervals. It runs in Unix-like systems and **relies on a file called the crontab (Cron table)** to manage scheduled jobs.

Let's say you love gardening and every evening you need to water your plants depending on soil levels. But it's all tiring; you are too lazy to do that manually, so you hire a friend for the task, say Alice. Alice here is your cron worker/daemon. Every day when the clock ticks sharp 6 PM, she starts checking the hydration level of each plant and waters them. Here, watering the plants was a job that needed to be done everyday at a specific time, so you assign a friend who does it for you, and that friend is what we are building.

## Basics

A Cron worker needs to know 3 main things:

- **Job:** What needs to be done (i.e., watering the plants in our example).
- **Trigger:** When it needs to be done (i.e., sharp at 6 PM).
- **End Time:** How long to continue doing this (i.e., infinitely / until stopped).

It is also helpful to know that there is a standard way most systems receive the Trigger and End Time as input as Cron Expression and Time ISO standard.

## The Anatomy of a Cron Expression

A standard cron expression consists of five fields separated by spaces (some systems, like Quartz or AWS, support a 6th or 7th field for seconds and years). Each field represents a specific unit of time:

![gemini-code-1780743011596.jpg](/projects/cron/gemini-code-1780743011596.jpg)

So an expression like every month at 12 PM is `0 12 1 * *`

### Breakdown of how this works:

- **`0`** (Minute): Exactly at the 0th minute.
- **`12`** (Hour): Exactly at 12 PM (using a 24-hour clock, 12 is noon).
- **`1`** (Day of the month): Exactly on the 1st day of the month.
- **`*`** (Month): Every month.
- **`*`** (Day of the week): Any day of the week.
